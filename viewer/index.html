What to read into this

  • ARCTIC is smooth but sits too deep. Motion-capture jitter is about 0.3 mm, yet the median contact frame is 3–4 mm inside the object and that depth barely changes frame to frame. That pattern is a surface bias (markers sit only on the back of the hand), not noise. Its own fitting_err does not predict penetration (Spearman 0.003).
  • EPIC-Contact penetrates about as much at the median but with a heavy tail on pans, saucepans and cans, where generic category templates replace the real object. The per-frame tier is documented as broadcasting the central frame’s hand–object pose, yet the hand drifts 9–23 mm relative to the object within a clip (p50–p90 of 200 clips).
  • SHOW3D is both deep and noisy. It estimates hands (multi-view IK) and objects (FoundPose + GoTrack) independently, with no temporal smoothing. The median contact frame is 5.2 mm inside the object, and 53% of frames are more than 5 mm in (ARCTIC: 26%). Jitter against the object is 1.3 mm median, about 4× ARCTIC’s. In 16% of frames the penetration depth jumps by more than 2 mm between frames. Fewer than 1% of frames are sim-clean. Left and right hands look alike (median 5.4 vs 5.1 mm).
  • HOT3D ships two hand fits per frame: the UmeTrack mesh its optical mocap actually tracks, and a MANO fit to it. Scored against UmeTrack, HOT3D looks like ARCTIC: median penetration 3.0 mm, 18% of contact frames more than 5 mm in, p90 6.2 mm, and jitter against the object 0.37 mm. Its MANO hands sit deeper on the very same frames: median 5.0 mm, 50% more than 5 mm in, about 1.6 mm deeper per frame. That fits MANO fingertips landing about 6 mm from UmeTrack’s. Aria and Quest 3 clips agree to within 0.3 mm. The worst case is the 5 lb dumbbell (median 7.6 mm even with UmeTrack). Hand-frames are counted per (hand, object) pair, using 2,748 pairs from 2,804 training clips (150 frames at 30 fps). Object poses are mocap with scanned meshes.
  • By hand part. ARCTIC’s overlap sits on the thumb and palm, which together hold 64% of its penetrating volume; the thumb is more than 2 mm inside in 53% of contact frames. About 70% of the volume is on the palmar side, consistent with a fit that is accurate on the marker-covered back of the hand but whose palm and finger pads sink into the object. HOT3D’s MANO hands put 51% of the volume on the thumb and 25% on the index finger. SHOW3D’s overlap is spread over the four fingers, with middle and ring deepest (p90 10–11 mm) and the palm minor (6% of volume). EPIC-Contact peaks at the index finger. In every dataset the back of the hand is also often inside (45–73% of frames over 2 mm). That cannot come from skin compression; it means whole finger segments pass into the object.
  • Left hands. SHOW3D fits left hands with the corrected MANO left model (with its shape-direction sign fix). The audit uses the same model, and with it, fingertips match SHOW3D’s own hand landmarks to about 4 mm. ARCTIC’s released code uses the stock left model, so ARCTIC is evaluated that way too.
  • Hands are rigid skinned meshes. A few millimetres of “penetration” in a firm grasp can be real skin compression, but a persistent multi-millimetre overlap is still force a rigid simulator will resolve as push-out.
  • Meshes: ARCTIC objects are the released watertight templates articulated per frame; SHOW3D and HOT3D use the HOT3D BOP meshes; EPIC-Contact uses its shipped per-clip meshes. Sign uses the generalized winding number, which tolerates non-watertight scans.
